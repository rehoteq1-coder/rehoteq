The Rehoteq AI Curriculum for Nigerian Schools: P1 to SSS 3

A free weekly scheme of work for teaching AI from Primary 1 to SSS 3, built on the Nigerian 13-week term, with an unplugged option for schools without a computer lab.

By Rehoteq Editorial TeamLast updated: 28 October 2026
About the author
Rehoteq Editorial Team produces practical explainers for Nigerian households, schools and small organisations. Read our editorial policy.

What this curriculum is

A free, complete scheme of work for teaching artificial intelligence and digital skills from Primary 1 to SSS 3, written for Nigerian schools and published by Rehoteq as two downloadable PDFs. It is organised the way Nigerian schools actually work: one topic per week, subtopics split across the periods, terms of thirteen weeks. The AI track is the first of several; tracks for data analysis, web development, graphic design, automation and cloud engineering follow.

Built on the Nigerian school year

Each term is a 13-week term. Weeks 1 to 10 carry the teaching: one topic per week, two periods, with period one introducing the concept and period two for practice. Week 11 is examinations. Weeks 12 and 13 are marking, computation and results, with first-term closure around 16 or 17 December. Because every SSS 3 student writes WAEC, NECO or NABTEB at the end of the third term, and JSS 3 students sit BECE, those two third terms are built around the external examination calendars rather than a normal teaching schedule.

Download the scheme of work

  • Primary edition (P1 to P6), PDF - three bands, every week with topics, period-split subtopics and instructional materials, including an unplugged option for schools without a computer lab.
  • Secondary edition (JSS 1 to SSS 3), PDF - a six-year pathway from Scratch to Python, data analysis and machine learning, aligned to WAEC and NECO computer studies.

What a school needs

The primary kit is deliberately low-cost: charts, pictures, flash and situation cards, solid objects such as bottle tops, beads and building blocks, and devices, where one shared phone or tablet can serve a small group. Every lesson has an unplugged fallback, so a school with no computer lab still teaches the concept; the device-based activity runs as a demonstration on the teacher's phone. The secondary pathway assumes increasing access to computers, and names free tools and cloud tiers throughout.

Nigerian context, global standards

Every level anchors its examples in Nigerian life: POS machines and bank fraud alerts, market and farm data, Lagos traffic, local languages. On the other side, the same foundations taught worldwide are mapped in explicitly: Scratch and Scratch Jr in primary, Python, pandas, scikit-learn, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, SQL, Git and cloud free tiers in secondary, aligned to the CSTA K-12 strands, with WAEC and NECO topics audited in SSS 3.
