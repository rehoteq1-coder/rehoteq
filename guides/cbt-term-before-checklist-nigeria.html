Running CBT in a Nigerian School: A Term-before Walkthrough

Plan a small internal computer-based examination from candidate numbers and power resilience to a rehearsed failure policy.

Rehoteq Editorial Team · Updated 23 September 2026 · Editorial policy

Buying computers is not the same as being ready for an examination. A school can have enough chairs and still lose time to shared passwords, mismatched candidate lists, failing batteries or uncertainty about what happens when a session stops. Readiness means the staff can run a fair exam, preserve the work and explain their decisions afterwards.

This lesson is a suggested sequence for internal school CBT. It does not accredit a centre or replace JAMB or another examination body’s rules. Our illustrative school has 120 candidates and hopes to use a 30-seat room. The dates below are a local planning framework, not an official Nigerian examination timetable.

Term start: agree what is being assessed

Write a one-page assessment brief: subjects, candidate groups, duration, question types, permitted materials, accessibility needs and marking responsibilities. Decide whether the examination runs on a local server, a managed online service or individual devices. Each choice creates different requirements for connectivity, account control, backup and support. “The internet works in the staffroom” is not a load test for a full class.

Appoint named roles rather than relying on one enthusiastic ICT teacher. The exam lead approves the paper and contingencies; the technical lead checks devices and recovery; invigilators supervise candidates; the result custodian controls exports and amendments. One person may hold multiple roles in a small school, but approval and checking should not disappear into a single undocumented action.

Use the existing CBT readiness checklist as the evidence sheet. For each item, record an owner, a due date and proof of completion. “Tested” should mean an observable result, such as a saved response surviving a controlled restart in a dummy session, not an assurance that the software normally works.

Eight weeks before: count usable seats and time

Suppose four of the 30 machines are reserved as tested spares. There are 26 scheduled candidate seats, so 120 ÷ 26 requires five sessions, rounded up. The fifth session has 16 candidates. Scheduling four sessions simply because 120 ÷ 30 = 4 leaves no practical room for a failed machine unless there is another tested arrangement.

For a 60-minute paper, allow an illustrative 20 minutes per session for identity checks, seating, login and instructions, plus 15-minute changeovers between sessions. Five × 80 minutes + four × 15 minutes = 460 minutes, or 7 hours 40 minutes, before staff breaks, unexpected delays or accommodations. That is an operational warning: consider more time or another day rather than removing checks to make the arithmetic fit.

Capacity is also about people. Can candidates enter and leave without seeing another group’s screens or discussing a reused paper? Who supervises waiting candidates? Does the room remain usable during rain or heat? Document these constraints before promising a timetable to parents.

Six weeks before: test power and the delivery platform

Inventory each device with a school asset identifier, operating system, supported browser, working keyboard, mouse, screen and charger. Use dummy identities. Check login, navigation, answer changes, autosave, final submission and the confirmation shown to the invigilator. A candidate reaching the last question does not prove that the server recorded a completed attempt.

Test the intended number of simultaneous users in a supervised mock. Observe login delays, reconnect behaviour and what happens when a session is resumed. Ask the platform supplier for its documented recovery and backup process. If it requires continuous internet, a local Wi-Fi network alone will not satisfy that requirement; if it runs locally, losing internet should not be assumed harmless without testing.

Have an electrical professional inspect the supply, distribution, earthing and backup arrangements. The battery/inverter learning tool can help prepare a load list, not approve a computer-lab installation. For example, 26 terminals at an assumed 50 W, plus 100 W of network/server equipment, total 1,400 W. An hour is 1.4 kWh before losses. Use measured equipment values and the required shutdown window for the actual design.

Professional support is required for mains circuits, generator transfer, UPS installation and load testing that involves electrical hazards. Do not pull a live supply or create a fault during an occupied examination. Conduct recovery exercises with dummy attempts under an approved, controlled plan.

Four weeks before: approve the paper and protect records

Have a second subject teacher review every question, accepted answer, mark allocation, image and special character. Preview the paper on the actual candidate devices, not just the author’s large monitor. Check that scrolling does not hide options and that a diagram remains legible. Confirm the configured duration and whether navigation rules match the instructions candidates receive.

Create a controlled candidate list and reconcile it with class records. Do not put students’ real names, dates of birth or login secrets into a public practice site to test the process. Give staff the least access they need, avoid shared administrator accounts, and document when exports are retained or deleted. Backups containing results remain personal data and need the same care as the live system.

Our Teacher Resource Portal helps with preparation, while the digital-results guide covers controlled publication. Neither replaces the school’s approved assessment or data-protection policy. Public calculators should receive fictional data or de-identified totals only.

Two weeks before: rehearse the candidate journey

Run a short, low-stakes mock using a different paper. Start outside the room: identification, seating, login, instructions, starting the timer, flagging questions, reviewing answers and submitting. Include candidates who need accessibility adjustments in planning rather than discovering an obstacle on exam day. Staff must know which accommodations are authorised and how they are applied consistently.

The Pre-JAMB CBT practice tool can familiarise learners with answering on screen. It is not proof that the school’s actual exam platform or room is ready, and its practice result is not an official JAMB score. Rehearsal on the delivery system remains essential.

Measure where the mock slows down. If ten students need password resets, the solution is a tested login-support process, not blaming them on exam day. Record the fault, action, responsible person and successful retest. Keep spare input devices and an approved seating-change process available.

One week before: decide failure rules in writing

Agree what happens after a failed terminal, a power loss, lost connectivity, an accidental submission or an allegation of misconduct. The exam lead should approve any pause, relocation, extra time or resit policy before seeing who is affected. A technician’s convenience should not determine candidates’ marks or time allowance.

A useful incident record contains the session identifier, time noticed, affected asset and candidate identifiers, observed symptoms, authorised action and time restored. Do not put passwords in the record. Restrict access and retain only as long as the approved policy requires. Inform students beforehand how to call an invigilator without leaving their seat or shouting across the room.

Test restoration from a backup in a separate controlled environment. An export file existing on a USB drive is not proof it can restore a session. Confirm who can contact the supplier and what evidence to provide without exposing the entire candidate database.

Exam day and the result handover

Use a signed opening checklist: room, devices, clock, approved paper version, candidate list, account access and backup status. Freeze unplanned software updates and paper edits through the school’s change-control process. Staff should not improvise a new grading rule because a score looks surprising.

After each session, reconcile expected candidates against recorded submissions before clearing the room for the next group. Export through an authorised account, preserve the original and have a second person check totals before publication. The GPA calculator demonstrates weighted points for tertiary study; it is not a school-exam grading engine. Use your approved scheme for real results.

Finish with a short review: usable seats, incidents, recovery time, disputed records and changes for next term. Keep the evidence local and private. Publish a useful readiness story only with consent and anonymisation, not screenshots exposing children’s data.

Frequently asked questions

Can this checklist make us a JAMB-accredited centre?

No. Internal CBT readiness and official centre accreditation are different matters. Use current JAMB instructions and its official channels for external examinations.

Must every CBT system be online?

No. Delivery models vary, but offline claims must be verified on the actual platform. Updates, licensing, identity checks or reporting may still require connectivity.

What if there are too few usable computers?

Reduce scheduled seats, add time or obtain approved additional capacity. Do not quietly remove spares, shorten candidate time or compromise supervision to preserve an unrealistic timetable.
