Method, scope and source table

Single-phase copper/PVC circuits only. Load current = electrical input watts ÷ (voltage × power factor). We test the listed sizes against current capacity and your run-drop budget, then show the first candidate. This does not select a breaker.

Capacity values below are Schneider’s IEC-based Figure G20, copper, 70°C PVC, 30°C ambient, three loaded conductors. Using them for a two-loaded-conductor single-phase estimate is deliberately conservative within these reference methods; it does not cover every installation. B2 means multicore cable in conduit on a wooden/masonry wall; C means cable clipped directly under the reference conditions. Neither option covers a cable surrounded by roof insulation, buried underground, in sunlight or on a hot roof.

Nigerian ambient conditions: measure the route

The 40°C example is an assumption, not a national design temperature. The temperature beside a roof cable may differ substantially from a weather forecast. An electrician must establish the worst credible route temperature, insulation and grouping. If your route is not represented, stop here and use the manufacturer’s data and a site design.

Worked example: 3 kW electrical input

At 230 V and PF 1, 3,000 ÷ 230 = 13.04 A. For B2, 40°C and one circuit, 1.5 mm² gives 15 × 0.87 = 13.05 A, but its 30 m estimated drop is 12.37 V (5.38%), exceeding the chosen 3% budget. 2.5 mm² also exceeds that budget. 4 mm² gives 23.49 A corrected capacity and about 4.64 V (2.02%) drop, so it is this model’s candidate, not an installation recommendation. A “3 kW AC” cooling rating is not necessarily 3 kW of electrical input.

Voltage-drop and protection limits

Run drop uses Figure G29: ΔV = 2 × I × L/1000 × [(23.7/S) × PF + 0.08 × √(1−PF²)]. L is one-way metres; S is mm². The published 23.7 Ω·mm²/km resistance approximation is conservative for the 70°C PVC capacity model; it is not measured cable resistance. Add upstream drops separately.

A professional must still coordinate design current, protective-device rating and corrected cable capacity, verify disconnection time, fault withstand, earthing, terminal limits, harmonics and motor starting. Repeated trips are never a reason to enlarge a breaker casually. We deliberately stop at 35 mm²; no match means no recommendation.
