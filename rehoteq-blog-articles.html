Article 2 — Education

5 Reasons Every Nigerian Secondary School Needs a Parent Portal in 2025

Category: Education | Reading time: 3 mins | Author: Rehoteq Technologies

Parent involvement is one of the strongest predictors of student academic success. Yet in many Nigerian schools, communication between school and home remains limited to end-of-term report cards and occasional PTA meetings. A parent portal changes this completely. Here are five compelling reasons why every secondary school in Nigeria should have one.

1. Real-Time Academic Updates
With a parent portal, parents no longer have to wait until the end of term to know how their child is performing. They can check results, see individual subject scores broken down by CA1, CA2, and examination, and identify areas where their child needs extra support — all from their smartphone.

2. Instant Fee Payment Status
Fee payment disputes are one of the most common sources of friction between schools and parents. A parent portal shows exactly what has been paid, when it was paid, and what is outstanding. Parents can even make payments directly through the portal, reducing queues at the bursar's office.

3. Attendance Tracking
Knowing when your child is absent from school is critical. A parent portal that shows daily attendance records allows parents to quickly identify patterns of truancy and address them before they become serious academic problems.

4. Direct School Communication
Important notices, announcements, and messages from the principal or class teacher can be delivered directly to parents through the portal, eliminating the risk of messages being lost in a child's school bag.

5. Assignment Visibility
When parents can see what assignments have been given and when they are due, they are better equipped to support their children's learning at home. This simple feature can significantly improve homework completion rates.

Solar Power for Nigerian Homes and Businesses: What You Need to Know in 2025

Category: Engineering | Reading time: 4 mins | Author: Rehoteq Technologies

Nigeria's electricity challenge is well documented. With grid power available for an average of just a few hours per day in many parts of the country, solar photovoltaic systems have moved from a luxury to a practical necessity for homes, businesses, and institutions that cannot afford the fuel costs of diesel generators.

The solar energy market in Nigeria has matured significantly over the past five years. Panel prices have fallen dramatically, battery storage technology has improved, and certified engineers with hands-on installation experience are increasingly available outside the major cities. For communities in Ondo State and across the South-West, solar is now a genuinely viable alternative to generator dependency.

Understanding the Components
A basic solar installation consists of four main components: solar panels (which convert sunlight to electricity), a charge controller (which regulates the charging of batteries), a battery bank (which stores energy for use at night or on cloudy days), and an inverter (which converts stored DC power to the AC power your appliances use).

Sizing Your System
The first step in any solar installation is an energy audit — calculating how much electricity you actually consume and when you consume it. A certified solar engineer will assess your load profile and recommend a system size that meets your needs without over-investing in capacity you will not use.

Why Certification Matters
Installing a solar system incorrectly can result in fire hazards, battery damage, and equipment failure. Always work with a certified electrical and solar PV engineer. Article 4 — Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity Basics Every Nigerian Student and Professional Should Know

Category: Cybersecurity | Reading time: 3 mins | Author: Rehoteq Technologies

As more Nigerians move their lives and businesses online — from mobile banking to WhatsApp business and social media marketing — the risks of cybercrime have grown alongside the opportunities. Nigeria consistently ranks among the most targeted countries for online fraud, phishing, and data theft. Yet basic cybersecurity awareness remains low among students, small business owners, and everyday internet users.

The Most Common Threats
Phishing attacks — where criminals send fake emails or messages pretending to be banks, government agencies, or well-known companies — remain the most prevalent form of cybercrime targeting Nigerian users. The goal is always the same: to trick you into revealing your password, PIN, or financial details.

Password Hygiene
Using the same password across multiple accounts is one of the most dangerous habits in digital life. If one account is compromised, attackers will immediately try the same password on your email, banking, and social media accounts. Use a unique, strong password for each important account, and consider a password manager to help you keep track.

Two-Factor Authentication
Enabling two-factor authentication (2FA) on your important accounts adds a critical layer of security. Even if someone knows your password, they cannot access your account without the second factor — typically a code sent to your phone.

Public WiFi Risks
Avoid accessing sensitive accounts — banking, email, school portals — on public WiFi networks. If you must use public WiFi, ensure the websites you visit use HTTPS (look for the padlock icon in your browser).

Article 5 — Education Tech

CBT Examinations in Nigerian Schools: Benefits, Challenges, and Best Practices

Category: Education Technology | Reading time: 4 mins | Author: Rehoteq Technologies

Computer-Based Testing (CBT) has been a feature of major Nigerian examinations — JAMB UTME, WAEC, and NECO — for over a decade. Yet the adoption of CBT for internal school examinations remains uneven, particularly in secondary schools outside the major urban centres. As device availability increases and internet access improves, this is changing rapidly.

Why CBT Matters for Schools
Traditional paper-based examinations are expensive to administer. Printing costs, physical storage of scripts, manual marking, and the risk of question paper leakage all add to the burden. CBT addresses each of these challenges. Questions are stored securely in a digital bank, each student can receive a randomised set of questions reducing copying, and results are generated automatically the moment an examination is submitted.

Preparing Students for National Examinations
Students who regularly practice in a CBT environment perform significantly better in JAMB and WAEC examinations than those encountering the format for the first time on examination day. Incorporating CBT into routine school assessments is one of the most effective ways to prepare students for the high-stakes national examinations that determine university admission.

Implementation Challenges
The main barriers to CBT adoption in Nigerian schools are device availability, reliable electricity, and technical support. Schools with computer laboratories are well-positioned to implement CBT immediately. For schools without dedicated labs, bring-your-own-device approaches using student smartphones have proved effective.
