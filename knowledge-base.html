AGM

Definition: Absorbent glass mat: a lead-acid battery construction that holds electrolyte in a glass-fibre separator.

In practice: Compare its permitted depth of discharge, charge profile and rated discharge time before choosing backup storage.

Example: A nominal 12 V, 100 Ah battery stores about 1.2 kWh; at a 50% planning DoD that is 0.6 kWh before conversion losses.

Common mistake: Treating “sealed” as permission to ignore ventilation or the manufacturer’s installation instructions.

Ampere (A)

Definition: The unit of electrical current: the rate at which electric charge flows.

In practice: Cable and protective-device decisions depend on current as well as installation conditions.

Example: A 2,300 W single-phase input at 230 V and PF 1 draws approximately 10 A.

Common mistake: Using W ÷ V for an AC load while silently assuming power factor is always one.

Amp-hour (Ah)

Definition: A measure of charge capacity, not energy by itself.

In practice: State bank voltage alongside Ah when comparing batteries.

Example: 48 V × 100 Ah = 4,800 Wh nominal; 12 V × 100 Ah = 1,200 Wh nominal.

Common mistake: Adding Ah across a series string as though the batteries were in parallel.

BMS

Definition: A battery management system monitors specified battery conditions and can command charge or load disconnection.

In practice: Check battery, BMS and inverter compatibility, including continuous and surge-current limits.

Example: A nominal 48 V pack supplying 2,400 W at 90% inverter efficiency would deliver about 55.56 A before voltage sag.

Common mistake: Assuming sufficient kWh automatically means the BMS can supply a high-power inverter.

CBT

Definition: Computer-based testing: an assessment delivered and answered using a computer system.

In practice: Readiness includes candidate identity, accessibility, saved responses and recovery—not only devices.

Example: 120 candidates with 26 usable scheduled seats require ceil(120 ÷ 26) = 5 sessions.

Common mistake: Treating an internal mock as proof of official examination-centre accreditation.

CGPA

Definition: Cumulative grade point average: total included quality points divided by total included credit units.

In practice: Use the institution’s approved inclusion and repeat rules.

Example: 120 prior quality points plus 23 new points over 30 + 6 units gives 143 ÷ 36 = 3.97 when displayed to two decimals.

Common mistake: Averaging semester GPAs without weighting their unit totals.

Changeover

Definition: A source-transfer arrangement that selects the intended electrical supply and prevents unsafe source interconnection.

In practice: Ask a qualified professional to explain isolation, interlocking, protection and normal operation.

Example: Two available sources do not mean two sources may be connected together; an ordinary standby arrangement supplies the intended load from one source at a time.

Common mistake: Using a generator lead through a wall socket or relying on memory instead of appropriate interlocking.

Depth of discharge (DoD)

Definition: The fraction of a battery’s nominal capacity removed during discharge.

In practice: Use manufacturer limits and a lifecycle plan rather than assuming all capacity is routinely available.

Example: A 5 kWh nominal battery at 80% planning DoD offers 4 kWh before inverter losses.

Common mistake: Confusing an 80% DoD with an 80% state of charge; they describe different remaining-energy positions.

Derating

Definition: Reducing a reference equipment capacity to account for actual service conditions.

In practice: Cable ambient temperature and grouping affect current capacity.

Example: A 27 A reference cable capacity × 0.87 temperature factor × 0.8 grouping factor becomes 18.79 A.

Common mistake: Applying a correction factor to a table for a different insulation or installation method.

Earthing

Definition: The connection of specified conductive parts to earth as part of an electrical safety arrangement.

In practice: A professional must assess earthing, bonding and protective-device operation together.

Example: A record showing one earth electrode does not establish a safe fault path: verification requires the appropriate measured tests, not an electrode count.

Common mistake: Assuming a visible earth rod alone proves that every circuit is safe.

Related lesson or tool → · Back to A–Z

Efficiency

Definition: Useful output divided by input, stated for particular operating conditions.

In practice: Include conversion losses in an energy budget and check partial-load behaviour.

Example: Delivering 900 Wh through an inverter at 90% efficiency requires approximately 1,000 Wh input.

Common mistake: Applying the same loss twice when an overall system factor already includes it.

Related lesson or tool → · Back to A–Z

GPA

Definition: A credit-weighted grade point average for a defined set of courses, often one semester.

In practice: Check grade boundaries and failed-course treatment in the academic handbook.

Example: Three units at grade point 5 and two units at grade point 4 produce (15 + 8) ÷ 5 = 4.60.

Common mistake: Treating a missing mark as zero or dropping a failed course from attempted units without authorisation.

Inverter

Definition: Equipment that converts DC electrical input into AC output within specified limits.

In practice: Match continuous W, VA, surge capability, battery voltage and supported charging features.

Example: A 1,000 W load at PF 0.8 represents 1,250 VA before any planning margin.

Common mistake: Choosing solely by the large VA number on a carton while ignoring continuous watts and thermal limits.

Kilowatt-hour (kWh)

Definition: A unit of energy equal to one kilowatt sustained for one hour.

In practice: Use kWh for runtime, storage and energy consumption; use kW for power.

Example: A 60 W fan used for 10 hours consumes 600 Wh, or 0.6 kWh.

Common mistake: Saying “a 5 kW battery” when discussing how many hours it can supply a load.

Load audit

Definition: A structured record of appliances, input power, runtime, priority and simultaneous-use assumptions.

In practice: Separate essential outage loads from everything installed in the building.

Example: Six 10 W lamps used for five hours consume 6 × 10 × 5 = 300 Wh.

Common mistake: Using air-conditioner cooling capacity as though it were electrical input power.

Related lesson or tool → · Back to A–Z

MPPT

Definition: Maximum power point tracking: a controller function that seeks useful operating power from a PV array within its limits.

In practice: Match array voltage and current to the equipment’s specified operating and absolute limits.

Example: Two hypothetical 40 V operating-voltage modules in series would total about 80 V at that operating point; cold open-circuit voltage is a separate check.

Common mistake: Using operating voltage to approve a string without checking cold open-circuit voltage. No connection is prescribed here.

Noindex

Definition: A robots directive asking supporting search engines not to include a page in search results.

In practice: Use it for public utility pages that should not compete in search; keep them out of the sitemap.

Example: If 44 pages are intended for indexing and 4 utility pages are not, the sitemap should contain those 44 intended URLs, not 48.

Common mistake: Treating noindex as access control; a public noindex page is still accessible.

Peak-sun-hours

Definition: Daily solar irradiation expressed as equivalent hours at 1,000 W per square metre.

In practice: Use location, season, orientation and shading evidence to estimate PV yield.

Example: 1 kWp × 4 illustrative peak-sun-hours × 0.70 overall delivery factor gives 2.8 kWh in that model.

Common mistake: Counting every daylight hour as an hour of full rated panel output.

Phishing

Definition: Deceptive messages or sites that seek sensitive information, payments, access or harmful actions.

In practice: Independently verify high-risk requests instead of trusting familiar names or urgency.

Example: A 30-second pause can identify the requested action; verification may take longer than 30 seconds.

Common mistake: Treating a clean link scan or HTTPS padlock as proof that a payment request is genuine.

Related lesson or tool → · Back to A–Z

Power factor

Definition: Real power in watts divided by apparent power in VA for an AC load.

In practice: Use the input data of the actual equipment when translating watts to current or VA.

Example: 1,000 W ÷ 0.8 PF = 1,250 VA; at 230 V single-phase that is about 5.43 A.

Common mistake: Confusing electrical power factor with inverter efficiency or a motor’s mechanical efficiency.

Quality points

Definition: Credit units multiplied by grade points, summed across included courses.

In practice: Keep exact point totals when calculating a new cumulative average.

Example: A 3-unit course with grade point 4 contributes 12 quality points.

Common mistake: Reconstructing exact points from a rounded CGPA when transcript totals are available.

Related lesson or tool → · Back to A–Z

Source

Definition: The origin of information or evidence used to support a claim; in electrical work, the origin of a supply.

In practice: For a lesson, check the regulator or manufacturer; for a circuit, identify every energising supply.

Example: Two articles repeating the same unsupported statement are not two independent confirmations.

Common mistake: Confusing a cited general principle with evidence that a named professional reviewed this page.

Related lesson or tool → · Back to A–Z

Surge / starting demand

Definition: A short-duration demand above an appliance’s ordinary running requirement.

In practice: Compare the appliance start profile with inverter or generator capability and battery limits.

Example: A hypothetical load drawing 500 W normally but 1,500 W during a brief start has a 3× starting demand; this is not a universal motor multiplier.

Common mistake: Assuming a 25% continuous-power margin covers every compressor or pump start.

Related lesson or tool → · Back to A–Z

Two-step verification

Definition: An additional verification step that helps protect account access beyond the initial sign-in mechanism.

In practice: On WhatsApp, keep the verification PIN and recovery email under your control and review linked devices.

Example: A registration code and a two-step-verification PIN are two different secrets; neither should be sent to a person asking in chat.

Common mistake: Assuming an extra PIN makes it safe to approve an unsolicited device-linking request.

Related lesson or tool → · Back to A–Z

Voltage drop

Definition: The reduction in voltage along a loaded circuit due to conductor impedance.

In practice: Budget the whole supply route and check steady operation separately from startup.

Example: With the site model, 20 A along 30 m of 4 mm² copper at PF 1 gives 7.11 V drop, 3.09% of 230 V.

Common mistake: Doubling a one-way route length before entering a calculator that already includes the return path.
