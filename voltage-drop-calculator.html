Voltage Drop Calculator — Single-phase Copper

Estimate voltage loss along a copper cable run, with explicit length, current, power-factor and supply assumptions.

Formula and assumptions

For a single-phase circuit, ΔV = 2 × I × L/1000 × (R × PF + X × √(1−PF²)). Drop % = 100 × ΔV ÷ supply voltage. Receiving voltage = supply voltage − drop. L is one-way metres; the factor 2 includes both conductors. R = 23.7/S Ω/km for copper and X = 0.08 Ω/km, using Schneider’s Electrical Installation Guide, G29. This is an approximate steady-load model, not a field measurement.

Only copper 1.5–35 mm², single-phase 100–260 V, lagging PF 0.5–1 are supported. The fixed published resistance assumption allows for warm conductors; actual resistance depends on temperature and cable construction. We do not support DC solar strings, aluminium, three-phase, leading power factor or unbalanced circuits here.

Worked example

20 A along 30 m of 4 mm² copper at PF 1 gives 2 × 20 × 30/1000 × 23.7/4 = 7.11 V. At 230 V this is 3.09%, just above a 3% example budget. Estimated receiving voltage is 222.89 V. Doubling the one-way length doubles the predicted drop; increasing copper area reduces the resistive part.

Budget the whole route

The default 3% is a user-editable budget, not a statement of Nigerian regulatory limits. If upstream wiring already loses 2%, a final run losing 3% does not make the whole path a 3% installation. An electrician should determine the applicable limit and appliance tolerance. Motor starting may produce a much larger temporary drop. Loose joints and a weak supply are not fixed by changing a number on this page.

Voltage drop does not prove cable safety. This tool makes no ampacity, protective-device, short-circuit or earthing check. A low drop can coexist with dangerous installation conditions. Do not probe a live panel to obtain inputs.
